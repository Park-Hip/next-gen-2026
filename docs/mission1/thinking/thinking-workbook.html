Company 101

Six things a working person already knows that make this problem make sense. Read these first; every exercise uses them.

Documents have types, numbers and dates

  • Common types: Nội quy lao động, Quy chế (for example quy chế lương thưởng), Quy định, Quy trình (step-by-step procedure), Hướng dẫn (guideline), Biểu mẫu (form), Thông báo (announcement), Quyết định (decision that issues or changes the others).
  • A formal document usually carries a number (số hiệu), a signing date, an effective date, and sometimes names the older document it replaces.
  • Watch for the vague clause "các quy định trước đây trái với quyết định này đều bãi bỏ". It does not say which documents are repealed, so someone has to work it out. ? common in Vietnamese administrative writing; look at a few public samples to confirm
  • Informal knowledge (FAQs, chat answers, slides) often has no number or date at all, which makes versions even harder to track.

Knowledge is scattered

  • Typical places: shared drives, wikis, email announcements, chat groups, HR software, and people's heads. ? general pattern, not Appota-specific
  • Typical mess: several copies of the same file, names like Quy che nghi phep_final_v2_moi.pdf, forwarded PDFs nobody updates, a pinned chat message from two years ago.
  • Today's fallback is "hỏi chị HR" or "hỏi anh ngồi cạnh". Fast for the asker, expensive for HR, and the answer depends on who you happened to ask.

Not everyone may see everything

  • Typical levels: Công khai nội bộ (whole group), Nội bộ đơn vị (one company or department), Hạn chế (certain roles), Mật (very few people). ? names vary by company
  • Usually restricted: salary bands and individual pay, performance reviews, disciplinary cases, board minutes, restructuring plans, customer data, and in a payments company, compliance and fraud procedures. ?
  • Information about an identifiable employee (pay, evaluation, health) is personal data. Vietnam's Personal Data Protection Law took effect on 01/01/2026, so access control is a legal matter, not just etiquette. ✓ Luật 91/2025/QH15 (read Điều 2 for exact definitions before citing it)

Who touches a document

  • Owner writes it (HR, legal, department head)
  • A director approves and signs
  • Someone publishes it somewhere
  • Employees read it, or fail to find it
  • HR or managers answer questions about it
The people who know which version is correct (owners) are not the people asking (employees). Your product sits between them.

How to learn this fast without having worked in a company

  • Ask 2-3 relatives or friends who work in offices the five questions on the right. 15 minutes each is enough. Write down what they actually say; never invent answers.
  • Read public labor-law Q&A pages (for example the hỏi đáp section of thuvienphapluat.vn). They are full of real employee questions about leave, OT and contracts. ? check it yourself
  • Read Appota's own public job posts. The AI intern post says the schedule includes 2 Saturdays a month. That is a real example of a detail that differs by unit and role. ✓ job post
  1. "Lần gần nhất anh/chị cần tra một quy định của công ty là gì?"
  2. "Anh/chị đã tìm ở đâu, mất bao lâu?"

Exercise 5 · 30 min · feeds Section 4, Q2, Q3

Decision log

25% of the score is decision quality. A good decision record has five parts: context, options, choice, reason and trade-off, and what would change my mind. The last one shows you can update when reality changes, which Mission 3 will probably test.

Worked example: where do we enforce access?

a. Filter before search (pre-filter)

Search only runs inside documents this person may see.

+ The LLM never sees forbidden text; easy to test with code. − Needs correct access metadata on every document.

b. Filter after search (post-filter)

Search everything, then remove forbidden results before the LLM.

+ Also keeps forbidden text from the LLM. − Forbidden documents can fill the top-k, leaving few or no good results.

c. Tell the LLM in the prompt

"Do not reveal confidential information."

+ Zero effort. − Prompt injection can break it; you can never prove zero leaks.

Choice: a. Reason: the model cannot leak what it never sees, and code can be tested exhaustively. Trade-off: metadata discipline. Would change my mind if: documents could not carry reliable access metadata, for example if permissions only existed as folder settings in a drive we could not read. (Then we would mirror folder permissions instead.)

Fill as many as you can. Empty decisions are fine; we can discuss them.

If you can't say how you'd measure "good", you can't prove your solution works. Here are the metrics in plain words.

Cut scope

An MVP tests the riskiest assumption with the least work. Here that assumption is: "We can give the right person the right, current answer without leaking anything." Anything that doesn't test this is plumbing you can fake (mock) for now.

Exercise 8 · 20 min · feeds Section 4, prepares Mission 3

Change scenarios

Mission 3 will probably change something on you. For each scenario, decide what kind of change it would be in your design:

Data

Edit a record or add a document. No developer needed. Best case.

Config

Change a rule setting (an access rule, a prompt template). Quick, low risk.

Code

New logic. Slower and riskier. A good design keeps this rare.

Your test set is your safety net: when something changes, add a test case for it first, then change data or config, then rerun all tests and show the before and after.
